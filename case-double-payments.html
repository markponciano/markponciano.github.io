Money found · Medical clinic

Are we paying anyone twice?

Some were. About A$8,000, found and recovered without anyone taking a sudden hit.

Result
About A$8,000 found and recovered
My role
Bookkeeper
When
2025

The situation

A busy medical clinic paid around 40 providers for the work they did. What each provider was owed came from the practice's records of the work they'd done.

Partway through the year the clinic changed how it paid them. A sensible change, but changeovers rarely happen on a single day.

What went wrong

For a few weeks the old way and the new way both ran, and some providers were paid twice.

Nobody noticed, because each payment looked perfectly normal on its own. It was only wrong when you put the two side by side.

What I did

  1. Put the payments next to the work. I cross-checked the payments export against the record of services actually delivered, so every payment had to answer to a piece of work.
  2. Pinned down exactly who, and how much. Not a rough estimate: each provider's real overpayment, worked out line by line. It came to about A$8,000 across 40 accounts.
  3. Recovered it gently. Rather than claw it back in one lump, I set it up as small instalments through the weekly clinic-fee invoice, so no provider took a sudden cut.

What changed

BeforeAfter
Overpayments nobody could seeFound, provider by provider

How it works for the technical reader

The clinic's payments export and the service export from its practice system describe the same work from two sides. During the changeover window, services that had been paid under the old method also appeared under the new one. Matching the two exports provider by provider showed every service paid more than once, and the total per account. The recovery was scheduled as a fixed deduction on each provider's weekly clinic-fee invoice until it was cleared, so it ran through the normal billing rather than as a separate demand.
