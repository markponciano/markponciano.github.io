Case Study

Reconciliation and controls for a busy AU superclinic

How I built the pipeline that reconciled about 15,000 transaction lines a month across nine payment types, and caught around A$8,000 the clinic was quietly overpaying its providers.

Mark Lorenze Ponciano · Systems, Automation & Bookkeeping for SMBs

The clinic

A confidential multi-practitioner medical superclinic in Australia. My job was the reconciliation behind the provider payments: matching what the clinic's practice-management system, Best Practice, said had happened against the payments that actually came in. The payment stream I owned covered around 40 providers, and the money arrived through nine different channels, each with its own rules.

The problem

On the surface it was bookkeeping. Underneath, it was a matching problem at volume, with no clean key to match on:

  • About 15,000 transaction lines a month, arriving across nine payment types — direct debit, EFT, Medicare, DVA, WorkCover, HICAPS, credit card, cash and deposit allocation — each reconciled by its own rules, with no shared ID across them.
  • WorkCover was the worst of it: high volume and mostly unreferenced, so there was often nothing clean to match a payment back to.
  • Best Practice doesn't export clean. None of it could be trusted or matched until the data was heavily normalised first.
  • The clinic changed how it paid providers partway through the year. The old and new methods overlapped for a stretch, and some providers were quietly being paid twice.

How I approached it

Same as always: I did the reconciliation by hand before I automated any of it. Working the real exports month to month is what showed me how each payment type actually behaved, which ones were the troublemakers, and exactly where the matching broke. Then I built for that, and left the genuinely ambiguous cases to human review rather than let a machine guess at real providers' money.

What I did

Foundation

Normalised the messy export first

Best Practice came out inconsistent and couldn't be matched as it was. I built the normalisation step that turned each export into one clean, consistent shape, because nothing downstream could be trusted until it was.

Automation

Built the reconciliation pipeline

An Apps Script pipeline that ran all nine payment types, each against its own matching rules. About 80% matched outright, another 15% part-matched for a quick human confirm, leaving only the hardest 5% for full manual reconciliation — the genuinely ambiguous cases like unreferenced WorkCover.

Accuracy

Reconciled to the cent, and it's provable

This isn't a "roughly right" number. Across about 15,000 lines a month, a single wrong match would surface as a break that stops the whole reconciliation from balancing. It balanced, every month. That's not a claim about the work; it's a property of it.

Found money

Caught about A$8,000 in double-payments

When the billing model changed and providers started being paid twice, I cross-checked the payments export against the service export to pin down exactly who had been overpaid and by how much. It came to roughly A$8,000 across 40 accounts.

Handled with care

Recovered it without anyone taking a hit

Rather than claw the money back in one lump, I set it up as small instalments through the weekly clinic-fee invoice, so no provider took a sudden cut to pay it back.

The results

80%matched automatically, before a human touched it
~A$8Kin double-payments caught and recovered
~15ktransaction lines reconciled a month

High-volume reconciliation across nine payment types that used to be a manual slog, now mostly automated and provably accurate. The money going out matched the work that came in, and an overpayment that would have gone unnoticed was found and put right without hurting a single provider.
