Systems that run themselves · Events caterer

Our tools never agree on what's booked

One record now holds what's true, and every other tool updates from it.

Result
Six tools, one record
My role
Systems & Automation Manager
When
Designed and built from June 2026

The situation

A booking was born in whichever tool the customer happened to touch. Some came through the website form, some through boards the team kept, some straight into an inbox, and some from marketing tools.

From there each tool kept its own copy: the CRM had one, the calendar another, the accounts a third. None of them had to agree, and nothing made them. Answering "is the 14th booked?" meant opening several screens and deciding which to believe.

What went wrong

Bookings, trailers and calendars didn't always agree, so a date could be double-booked or missed. Each one was somebody's job to keep up to date, and keeping up to date is exactly what people stop doing on a busy week.

The deeper problem was the stage a booking was in. It was a field someone typed. A booking could be paid in the accounts and still sitting in "awaiting payment" on the card, because updating the card was a separate act of memory.

What I did

  1. Mapped where a booking could be born. The website form, the boards, the inbox, the marketing tools. Every route in, written down.
  2. Picked one place to hold the truth. A database I designed, holding the booking itself: the customer, the date, the trailer, the money, and what has happened to it.
  3. Made every other tool follow it. The CRM, the accounts, the calendar, the email tool and the document tool are all updated from that record, so what they display always comes from the one place. Each connects the way it has to, and messages coming back in are signed so the system knows they're genuine.
  4. Stopped anyone setting the stage by hand. A booking's stage is worked out from what has actually happened: quote accepted, form in, invoiced, paid. There's no field to forget.
  5. Gave dates and trailers one home. A calendar built on the same record, so a trailer can't be promised twice.
  6. Checked the copies every night. A nightly pass compares the mirrors to the record and puts back anything that has drifted, so a missed update fixes itself instead of waiting to be noticed.

How it works for the technical reader

A MySQL schema I designed holds the booking and its facts. GoHighLevel, Xero, the calendar, Brevo and CloudConvert are mirrors, each with its own connection: a private integration token for the CRM, OAuth with rotating refresh tokens for Xero, API keys elsewhere, and HMAC signatures both to verify inbound webhooks and to sign the links sent to customers. Stage is derived from the facts on the booking rather than stored, so it cannot drift from reality; the one deliberate exception is delivery, which has to be attested by a human because it cannot be observed.

Outward writes are mirrored on every change, with a nightly sweep that re-reads the mirror and repairs anything missing. Writes that can be lost, such as a history note during a CRM outage, record what they wrote, so a nightly retry can finish the job rather than losing it for good. Every schema change is a numbered migration with the reason written into it.
