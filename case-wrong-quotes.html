Salespeople keep sending wrong quotes

Now the screen only offers the right next step.

Result
Four steps, nothing that breaks a quote
My role
Systems & Automation Manager
When
June 2026, first version in four days

The situation

Every booking's price lived in a row of fields that all looked the same. The team worked from their CRM, GoHighLevel. Salespeople filled in the fields on each booking's card, and three things read those fields: the quote, the invoice, and the accounts.

Then the pricing changed and got complicated: service charges, add-ons, extra hours. The salespeople were good at selling, not at invoicing or VAT, and the card gave them no help. The total sat in the same plain box as every field above it.

What went wrong

Quotes went out with wrong details. A wrong field didn't stay on the card. The general ledger believes what is happening inside the opportunity, so the mistake travelled into the quote, then the invoice, then the books.

What I did

  1. Found where it broke. Every wrong quote traced back to the same thing: a flat screen that offered every move, including the wrong ones, to people who weren't there to do bookkeeping.
  2. Wrote the plan first. The Console got its own chapter in the system's written spec before any code: give people a screen where the wrong move isn't offered, instead of training them around a fragile one.
  3. Built it. The Operator Console, a screen linked from every booking card. The first version was built in four days.
    • Four steps, in the order the work happens: quote, internal form, awaiting payment, paid. The current step is lit, and so is its one right action.
    • The quote is the invoice. The quote is shown itemised, and the total a salesperson approves is exactly the total invoiced. Nothing gets re-typed.
    • Choices, not free typing. Discounts are 0, 5, 10 or 15%. Add-ons are switches.
    • One way out. Cancel is the only other action on a live booking.
    • A safety net. Edits can be undone for 15 seconds, and every action is logged with who took it.

How it works for the technical reader

A standalone page inside WordPress, behind a login and a single permission that only the operator role and admins hold. It reads each booking from the MySQL database that every tool mirrors, and the booking's stage is worked out from what has happened, never set by hand. Approving a quote raises the Xero invoice from the same itemised lines the operator saw. Edits queue for 15 seconds before they send, so an undo never reaches the customer. Every action writes a row to an audit log. A link on each GoHighLevel card opens that booking's Console. It was rehearsed in a dry-run mode before the switch-over, so no real invoice or email went out until it was proven.
