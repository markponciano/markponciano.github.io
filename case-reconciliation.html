Numbers you can trust · Medical clinic (same client)

Reconciliation takes up someone's whole month

Now it's Monday morning, and the report goes out that afternoon.

Result
A whole month, down to Monday morning
My role
Bookkeeper
When
Monthly, through 2025

The situation

Every month, about 15,000 transaction lines had to be matched against the payments that actually came in. The clinic's practice system recorded the work; the money arrived through nine different channels.

Done by hand, that was my whole month. On top of it came a report every week, and an invoice to each GP for the clinic's share: a set percentage of the services they'd billed. All of it depended on the matching being right.

Each channel had its own rules, and there was no shared ID running through them to match on. Direct debit, EFT, Medicare, DVA, WorkCover, HICAPS, card, cash, and deposits each had to be worked a different way.

What made it hard

Some lines had nothing to match on at all. WorkCover was the worst: high volume and mostly without a reference, so there was often nothing clean to tie a payment back to.

And the practice system's export didn't come out clean. None of it could be matched reliably until it had been put into one consistent shape first.

What I did

  1. Did it by hand first. For months that was the whole job, and it taught me how each payment type actually behaved and where matching broke.
  2. Cleaned the export into one shape. A normalisation step turned each messy export into one consistent format, because nothing downstream can be trusted until that's done.
  3. Gave each payment type its own rules. An Apps Script pipeline worked all nine channels, each against the rules that fit it.
  4. Sorted the results by how sure it was. Matched outright, part-matched for a quick confirm, or left for a person. The genuinely ambiguous cases, like unreferenced WorkCover, stayed with a human rather than letting a machine guess at real providers' money.
  5. Moved the work to Monday. The engine did the matching; I worked the flagged lines on Monday morning and the weekly report went out that afternoon. The rest of the week went to other work. When I left, I handed it over with a walkthrough, so it didn't depend on me.

How it works for the technical reader

The practice system's export was normalised into a single schema first: consistent dates, amounts, provider identifiers and payment-type labels. Each of the nine payment types then ran through its own matching rules, since a Medicare line and a HICAPS line carry different evidence. Results were bucketed by confidence: exact matches cleared automatically, partial matches were queued for a human to confirm, and lines with nothing to match on went to full manual reconciliation. Because every line had to land in one of the three, a wrong match would show up as a break in the month's balance. It balanced every month.
