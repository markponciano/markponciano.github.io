Systems built · Café group

Supplier invoices eat hours of data entry

Now each bill routes itself to the right café's ledger, by its own rules.

Result
15–25 manual entries a day, gone
My role
Assistant Bookkeeper
When
2023 to 2024

The situation

A hospitality group ran five cafés, and each one was its own business in the accounts. Five separate Xero ledgers, each with its own suppliers, bank feeds and reconciliation.

This was my first role there: accounts payable across all five. Supplier bills arrived in the mailboxes, and every week I sent each one across by hand to the right café's Xero.

What made it hard

Five ledgers means five places an invoice can go, and only one of them is right.

Every bill was a small decision and a small manual job, 15 to 25 of them a day. Repetitive, and exactly the kind of work where one slip puts a bill in the wrong café's books.

What I did

  1. Did it by hand first. Every week, across all five cafés, until I knew which suppliers served which café and how their bills arrived. And until I'd had enough of it.
  2. Used the tools already there. No custom code: rules in the group's email and in Xero were enough to do the sorting. The right size for the job.
  3. Wrote a rule for each café. Each of the five Xero organisations got its own specific routing rules, so a bill only lands where it belongs. Accuracy first; the time saved came with it.

What changed

BeforeAfter
15 to 25 bills sent across by hand a dayRouted automatically

How it works for the technical reader

Each café was a standalone Xero organisation. Rules in Google Workspace identified supplier bills as they arrived in the mailboxes and passed each one to the Xero organisation it belonged to, where it landed ready for review. Each organisation had its own specific rules, written for accuracy. Nothing custom was built: the email and accounting tools the group already paid for did the routing. Reconciliation stayed per organisation, against each café's own bank feed.
